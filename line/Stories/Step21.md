### R1k-1 The intent key, the actor and the sequence

- **Outcome:** `IntentActor` (a readonly struct over a `ulong`, `None` is zero, equality, `ToString`) is the opaque id of whoever intends: Networking fills it from the authenticated participant, a single-player game from one value of its own; never a Steam type or a connection. `IntentKey` (a readonly struct: `IntentActor Actor`, `uint Sequence`; `None`; equality; `IsNone`) is twelve bytes on the wire: `IPacker` through Core's `Packer` (the `ulong` then the `uint`), `TryUnpack(ref ReadOnlySpan<byte>, out IntentKey)` that never throws, and `Write(ref BitWriter)` and `TryRead(ref BitReader, out IntentKey)` for a command written with the bit writer. `IntentSequence` (a sealed class: `IntentSequence(IntentActor actor)` and `IntentSequence(IntentActor actor, uint nextSequence)`, `Actor`, `NextSequence`, `IntentKey Next()`) is the per-actor counter a writer stamps with, the local counter a game with no network supplies; a sequence that reaches `uint.MaxValue` throws rather than wrapping. `IntentReceipt` (a readonly struct: `ObservationReason Reason`, `uint Count`; `None`) is eight bytes: what applying an intent answered, kept so a repeat gets the same answer. `CoreKit.Intents.Sequence(actor)` makes a sequence.
- **Rules:** no allocation in any pack or unpack; a key's bytes are written down in a golden byte test; sequence zero is never issued (`Next()` starts at 1 unless told otherwise).
- **Regression contract:** the step's.
- **Done:** tests on equality and `None`, the golden bytes both ways, `TryUnpack` on a short span, `TryRead` on a short bit span, the sequence's first key and order, the start value, the overflow, and `Pack_NoAllocation`.

### R1k-2 The ledger

- **Outcome:** `IntentLedger` (a sealed class) is one world's bounded window of applied intents: `IntentLedger(int windowSize, TickSpan departureGrace, ObservationContext observations)`; `IntentLedgerCheck Check(in IntentKey key, out IntentReceipt receipt)` answers `Unseen` (apply it), `Applied` (change nothing, answer the receipt) or `Stale` (older than the actor's window, refuse it); `bool Record(in IntentKey key, in IntentReceipt receipt)` records a receipt and answers false without overwriting when the key is already applied or stale; `int ActorCount`; `long Bytes` (the design figure: actors held times the window size times twelve bytes). Per actor a window of `windowSize` entries (sequence and receipt), keyed by sequence so out-of-order arrival is fine; an entry older than the highest sequence minus the window is stale. `IntentLedgerSettings` is a `ScriptableObject` under the asset menu Spellbound with the fields `windowSize` (default 32) and `departureGraceSeconds` (default 300), the defaults as static readonly fields on the type, implementing Core's authoring contract `IAuthoredDefinition` (`Validate(ValidationReport report)` refuses a window size under 1 and a negative grace). `CoreKit.Intents.CreateLedger(TickRate rate, ObservationContext observations)` and `CreateLedger(IntentLedgerSettings settings, TickRate rate, ObservationContext observations)` convert the grace seconds to ticks at the rate given, once, when the ledger is created. The ledger reports `core.intent.ledger_bytes` (a gauge), `core.intent.repeats` (a counter, a repeat answered from the window) and `core.intent.stale` (a counter), declared in `CoreObservations`.
- **Verified (2026-11-05):** `ObservationSettings` does not implement `IAuthoredDefinition`, and no type of Core's outside its test fixtures does, so `IntentLedgerSettings` is the first Core settings asset to implement it.
- **Rules:** the ledger lives in a world (no static), two ledgers never share a window; a window is allocated when an actor first records and nothing allocates on a record into an existing window; the window size and the grace are values, never constants in logic; the ledger knows no connection and no player type.
- **Regression contract:** the step's.
- **Done:** tests on unseen then applied then the same receipt back, a different receipt not overwriting, stale below the window, out-of-order sequences inside the window, two actors apart, two ledgers apart, the bytes figure, the counters and the gauge read from the context, `Record_NoAllocation` for an actor already present, the settings asset's defaults and a custom asset, and `default` of the check enum never meaning applied.

### R1k-3 Departure, return and the single-player shape

- **Outcome:** `Depart(IntentActor actor, Tick now)` marks the actor's window to drop at `now + departureGrace`; `Return(IntentActor actor)` cancels the drop so a reconnecting actor keeps the window; `int Expire(Tick now)` drops every window whose grace has passed and answers how many, counted under `core.intent.windows_dropped`; `Depart` marks and never drops; `Expire` is the only drop path. A record into a departed window before its drop cancels nothing (the actor is still gone) but is kept. A game with no network never calls `Depart`: a test shows the single-player shape, one `IntentSequence` and one ledger, a repeated key changing nothing. The connect-and-disconnect test: 2,000 actors connect, record and depart in turn with the clock advancing past the grace, on a window sized for 500, and `Bytes` never exceeds the figure for 500 actors plus those inside the grace; the full 20,000 on 5,000 soak is R1b's.
- **Rules:** the grace is ticks converted once at creation from seconds; a dropped window's memory is reused (a free list), so the soak allocates nothing after warm-up; `Expire` is called by whoever steps the world (the world instance from the clock's step, R1b and L3), never by a timer inside Core.
- **Regression contract:** the step's.
- **Done:** the tests above, `Expire_NoAllocation`, and the test that a `Return` after the drop starts an empty window.

- **Outcome:** `JoinScheduler` (joins and resyncs in one queue per world; bytes per tick per joiner and per authority; enters spread over ticks, nearest region first, up to the per-player enters budget; a per-sender resync cooldown); `JoinState` on a client (`Joining` buffering batches above S by sequence, `Live`, `Resyncing` dropping the region's state, asking for a fresh snapshot and rejoining through the paged route); the watchdog (a join that has not finished inside the watchdog ticks becomes a resync, so pending forever cannot occur); and the resync triggers (a sequence that is not the next and not in the ring, an unknown source, a source that throws on apply).
- **Rules:** the scheduler is per world instance, never static; every budget is a `ProtocolSettings` field; the states are a closed set and `default` is never `Live`.
- **Regression contract:** the step's.
- **Done:** tests on the queue's budgets per tick, nearest first, the cooldown, the watchdog, each trigger, the buffered batches applied in order after the last page, a duplicate ignored, and a restart of the authority with ten clients resyncing within the budget and never in one tick.

### NW1-6 The interest map and the region geometry

- **Regression contract:** the step's.

### NW1-7 The write policy and the owner-mode path

- **Outcome:** `WritePolicy` and `OperationRoute` are bound per source at `RegionHub.Bind(source, policy)` (the protocol's region: a `RegionHub` holding its sources, its ring, its pager and its sequence; sealed after binding; opening to an observer before sealing or sealing with no source is refused with one error line through Core's logger). The owner path: a writer's `WriteChanges` goes up in one upload, the authority applies owner records in arrival order to its copy and flushes them to the observers in the next batch; the writer's pending mask (a writer ignores an incoming record for a key while its own write of that key is not yet echoed). The host and server path: a command up (NW1-9), the batch back; `Host` and `Server` are one code path differing in what the audit expects.
- **Rules:** no privileged default; a policy left `Unset` at seal is an error; owner mode asks no validator, and decode and size limits still apply.
- **Regression contract:** the step's.
- **Done:** the two-owner simulator case (two owners, one key, reorders and delays by seed, equal digests at the end; the authority's arrival order is the final order), the pending mask keeping a quick second write from flickering, an owner's own echo changing nothing, a seal with no source refused, and an `Unset` refused.

### NW1-8 Leases, the lease table and the durable bound

- **Outcome:** `LeaseTable` (per world: lease to participant and the highest counter accepted from that lease; `Grant(participant)` through the issuer; `TryAccept(InstanceId id, ParticipantId sender)` accepts only an id whose lease the table issued to that sender with a counter above the last accepted, else a counted reason; never renumbers); `BlockLeaseIssuer : ILeaseIssuer` (Core's seam; grants from a reserved block of `leaseBlockSize`; writes the block's upper bound through `ILeaseRecordStore` before the first lease of the block is granted; on start its next lease is the recorded bound, so a block that did not finish is skipped and never reissued); `ILeaseRecordStore` (`bool TryReadBound(out int)`, `WriteBound(int)`; the default `MemoryLeaseRecordStore`; the Persistence-backed one is step 31's); `LeasedIdentityAllocator` (wraps Core's `LocalIdentityAllocator`; at `leaseCounterWarningFraction` of the counter it records `networking.lease.counter_warning` and takes the next lease: a roll, not a failure); and the observations `networking.lease.granted` and `networking.lease.rejected` (with a reason).
- **Verified (2026-11-05):** `LocalIdentityAllocator` takes a new lease only when it holds none or its counter passed `uint.MaxValue`, and exposes `CurrentLease`, `NextCounter` and `State`; nothing on it forces a new lease earlier, while a fresh `LocalIdentityAllocator(issuer)` takes one on its first `Allocate`. `InstanceId.Compose` refuses a lease under 1.
- **Rules:** a lease is issued once in a world and never reused, not after a disconnect, not after a restart; the layout of `InstanceId` is Core's and nothing here reads its bits except through `LeaseOf` and `CounterOf`.
- **Regression contract:** the step's.
- **Done:** tests on grant and accept, a forged lease, a counter not above the last, a replayed id, the bound written before the grant (a store that records the order), the restart skipping the unfinished block, the roll at the warning fraction, the custom store, and the crash-in-a-sliced-save simulator case (NW1-11 runs it; the mechanics are here).

### NW1-9 Commands, Core's intent key and Core's ledger

- **Outcome:** `CommandEnvelope` (the intent key, the source index, the command bytes; written with the bit writer, read bounded); `CommandLimits` from the settings (size per command, commands per sender per tick); the authority's command path for a source bound `Host` or `Server`: size, then rate, then the ledger (`IntentLedger.Check`: `Applied` answers the stored receipt and changes nothing, `Stale` refuses with the reason `intent.stale`), then `ICommandSource.TryApplyCommand(sender, key, ref reader, out receipt)`, whose receipt is recorded. The sender's `ParticipantId` comes from the edge, never from the payload; `ParticipantLeft(participant, tick)` calls `ledger.Depart` and `ParticipantReturned` calls `Return`. Networking keeps no window of its own: the world's `IntentLedger` is Core's, created by whoever creates the world, and the protocol takes it as an argument. The writer side stamps with Core's `IntentSequence` per participant.
- **Rules:** a repeat changes nothing (one test sends each command twice); a key from an actor other than the sender is refused (`intent.actor_mismatch`); every refusal is a counter under `networking.command.rejected` with its reason.
- **Regression contract:** the step's.
- **Done:** tests on each limit, the repeat, the stale, the mismatch, the departure and return against Core's ledger, and `Submit_NoAllocation` on the writer.

### NW1-11 The simulator and its cases

- **Outcome:** `ReplicationSimulation` in the Editor assembly: an authority and N clients in memory over `SimulatedNetwork` (drops, delays in ticks, duplicates and reorders, each a probability by seed through Core's `SeededStream`), each peer a world with its own `ObservationContext`, `TickClock` source, `IntentLedger`, lease table and hubs; a `SourceDigest` (an FNV hash over a source's state in key order) compared across peers; `SimulationResult` (per case: ticks run, bytes sent, batches, pages, joins, resyncs, commands applied and rejected by reason, digests equal or not, the worst tick). The cases, each a test and each runnable by seed: snapshot with unflushed changes; join race (joiners arriving while the authority flushes every tick, with catch-up from the ring, ring overrun to the paged route, a duplicate and a reorder on the way); hostile commands (oversized, over rate, malformed bytes, a forged sender's actor, a replayed key, a stale key, a client-named id outside its lease, a counter below the last accepted); two owners one key; transfer (observer of A only, of B only, of both, a joiner into B during the move, a walker crossing a border 100 times with the margin, two records per real crossing, equal state every tick); reference-frame-local partition (a rider from the world frame onto a ship frame); crash in a sliced save (kill after a slice holding rows under lease L and before the world saves again, restart, grant, mint, no minted id equals a saved one, slices and kill point varied by seed).
- **Rules:** the simulator uses no engine type beyond Core's and no coroutine; a case's figures are read by the operation of NW1-12 and go in the report as tables; 100 seeds per case in the suite's long form, 10 in the default run.
- **Regression contract:** the step's.
- **Done:** every case green across its seeds with the figures in the report; the hostile case shows zero hostile commands applied and equal digests; the join race shows every joiner live with equal digests and no join past the watchdog.
