## The ask

The world becomes a deterministic function of a seed and a definition. Everything that builds or draws the world reads that function at its own resolution through a seam its owner defines, so WorldForge never meshes, never draws and never knows what a voxel is. ## After

### The idea in one picture: one function, many readers

The world is a function from (seed, definition) to a shape. The shape is crossing surfaces (the developer's decision): a top surface, the ground, and for each air layer beneath it a floor and a ceiling. The open space of a column is the union of everything above the ground and every layer whose ceiling lies above its floor; where a floor rises to meet its ceiling the layer closes, and where two layers overlap they merge (the existing `UnderDarkCeiling <= UnderDarkFloor` check at `VoxelizeJob.cs:87` is one case of this). A layer thinner than a configurable minimum is dropped, because one signed byte per voxel cannot hold a paper-thin double surface. Operators then add or carve in three dimensions. Every reader samples that function with a footprint: the spacing at which it will use the answer. Fractal noise is cut to the octaves that spacing can represent, so a coarse reader sees the low-pass of what a fine reader sees and the two agree on everything coarse.

| Reader | Footprint | Package | What it takes from the model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voxel chunk | 1 unit | GeoForge, through the `WorldForge.GeoForge` bridge | Density and material, written into GeoForge's array |
| Far terrain | 4 to 64 units | Horizon | Height and class |
| Seed preview, minimap, world map | 16 to 512 units | The game, from WorldForge's raster | Height, class, labels |
| Scatter | the rule's cell | Instancing | Rules, and a scrolling grid of height and class around the camera, which is one `ISurfaceSource` request moved by an origin offset |
| Pathfinding cost | the planner's cell | Navigation | Height, class, occupancy |
| Chunk skipping | one chunk | GeoForge, Horizon | Empty, solid or mixed |

Today's far tiles sample the full four-octave noise at 32 units (`DistantTerrainManager.cs:18`, `TerrainNoiseSettings.Default`: frequency 0.006, 4 octaves, lacunarity 2). With those defaults the third and fourth octave have wavelengths near 42 and 21 units, below the 64 a 32-unit spacing can represent (computed, assuming a wavelength of about 1/frequency). That does not prove the pop-in the developer saw flying toward the islands (`Evidence.md`), but it is a difference in kind between near and far, not only in detail, and the footprint removes it by construction. The first Horizon story checks it with a capture.

### The pieces

**WorldModel (WorldForge).** The existing sampling, layout and plan code, rebuilt on plain native data. Changes, each answering a row of the Before: no Entities, no statics, no `Resources` (an instance holds its registries and takes them from the definition); an ordered list of surfaces replaces the five fixed fields; `seaLevel` and `UnderDark` become names in a definition (a surface named `water` of role label, a floor and a ceiling named by the game); sampling takes a footprint; every operator and rule declares its influence bounds (the README already wants this: "passes and content stages should declare the world-space dependency radius"), which gives the conservative occupancy classification; placement ids derive from the rule, the cell and the ordinal, never from a list position; a fingerprint of definition, seed and algorithm version is a Persistence section so a saved world knows which generation made it.

**Rules.** A rule says where in the world something is, never what it is. A placement rule: criteria, a weighted preset table, a cell size and jitter, emitting records. A scatter rule: the same, for Instancing. A place rule: a Place asset, a count, a spacing, criteria and an anchor (surface, absolute, or depth below a surface). A line rule: a route or ring, its corridor and its carve. Today's `BiomeObjectEntry`, `BiomeAssemblyScatterEntry`, `LocationDefinition` and `LineDefinition` are these, with content entries removed from locations (a place holds its own content).

**Labels.** A label is a named surface that does not change the shape. WorldForge answers "where does terrain fall below `water`"; whether that is water, lava or fog is the game's. This is the requirement-3 boundary of the WorldForge brief, and the underwater game defines no label at all. Evidence (2026-10-26), a data point and not a decision: a water rendering probe in a throwaway project read its shore depth from a height map captured from above; `Planning/PackageLine/Evidence/WaterProbe.md` in the Spellbound workspace.

**Horizon.** Draws terrain beyond the voxels from any `ISurfaceSource`, with a handover band at the edge of what `INearCoverage` reports. It depends on URP by its sentence and has no backend seam. The default source is a height array. Settings, all fields with defaults: view distance, handover mode (hide on coverage as today, or a dither band), band width, skirt depth, depth bias (today's `-2.5`), a palette asset mapping class bytes to colours or textures, and whatever the chosen renderer needs (a ring renderer would add ring count and cell size per ring). A budget declared through Core's observations. The renderer is a replaceable class and none is chosen: the candidates are under Directions E, W6.1 plans the measurement and W6.2 runs it.

**Places.** An authored unit of content: typed layers each stamped by its owner, parts composed by adjacency rules (Saboteer's six sockets and its solver move here), nesting, duplication that renames every reference, validation and preview through Core's authoring contract, a gym. Each layer owner implements both halves: stamp a layer into the world and capture a layer out of it, in the editor and in a running build, so a player's build can become a Place by the same code; a changed layer format means re-capture, with no version, reader or migration kept, and validation enforces size limits and schema checks before an untrusted asset is stamped. A Place keeps its layers in Unity's own serialization, as serialized fields of the asset: Unity's serializer survives a reordered field, and a renamed one is an error that `core validate` reports by name with the stored value text, and nothing is written anywhere to keep it. After a package has shipped to customers, renaming a serialized field of a Place or a layer is a breaking change and a major, as semantic versioning says, and the release check fails it. The voxel layer holds its runs as serialized fields per chunk-sized block (density, material, length), the shape the 51 capture assets already take; those are 63.7 MiB on disk today (counted: 863,953 run entries), so the reshaped layer is expected to be of the same order, and W4b's first layer story measures file size and import time before the layer type is frozen. Core packing is used only when a Place is saved by a running game: the Place packs to bytes as a section whose header holds its name, its layout fingerprint and its length, and unpacks to the same fields; at 6 bytes a run that is about 5.2 MB for the 51 captures (computed from the counts, not measured). A section holds no tick rate. **A layer whose owner is not installed** lives only in the asset, never in a pack: Unity serializes a managed reference by its stored type name, and when the type does not compile in a project (a scatter layer in Saboteer, which does not install Instancing) the reference reaches C# as null (`SerializationUtility.GetManagedReferencesWithMissingTypes` lists it; `ClearManagedReferenceWithMissingType` is the call that drops its data, Unity scripting reference, 6000.4). The Unity page does not say the data survives a save; its wording for the clear call implies it is kept until cleared, so W4a's first story proves it before anything rests on it, and until it passes the Place inspector shows a place with such a layer read-only. Sharing a place is a file in a package, so the layer travels as the asset's own text and Saboteer never needs to read it. A running Saboteer build holds only the layers it compiled, and a pack made in the editor of a place with such a layer is refused with `error places.layer-not-installed`, naming the layer, so a pack never drops one in silence. The `LayerOwnerInstalledCheck`, an `IDefinitionCheck<PlaceDefinition>` in the Places editor assembly registered as `places.layer-owner-installed (Places)`, lists each such layer as `information places.layer-carried layers[3]` with the stored class and assembly name, and the package when the assembly name starts with a package's assembly name. Core.Editor's authored-key scan skips a layer whose stored type is missing (it cannot know the type's keys) and reports it through that check, never as `core.unknown-key`; both are claims on area 2, and CL4 story 11 gains a missing-type fixture.

**GeoForge changes (its boundary only).** It stops at density in, meshes out. the chunk presenter opens (already L7) and splits in two, a collision part that compiles in every build (the march, the baked mesh and the `MeshCollider`) and a picture part under `!UNITY_SERVER`, because the only code that sets a collider's `sharedMesh` is `OctreeNode.cs:406`, inside the one presenter, so a headless server would have no terrain collider (WorldObjects' collision proxy follows the same split, L3); the manager stops being a process-wide singleton: it is an instance a group of volumes owns, passed to chunks and commands, and every query names its volume (today `GeoForgeManager` holds one `HashSet<IGeoVolume>` of every volume, `GeoForgeManager.cs:27`, answers `TryQueryVoxel(position)` across all of them, `:310`, and is fetched by `SingletonManager` in the chunks, `TerraformSphereCommand.cs:68` and `GeoForgeStatic.cs:21,30`); **Boards in Studio.Content.** The generators name no game: Studio.Content holds a cave rooms generator, a grid board generator and a ramp generator, and Studio.Content never names a game. **The designer's terrain.** Today a point of interest's terrain is shaped in play mode: `SaboteerPoiGenerationController` digs and adds with keyboard spheres and appends every edit to `SculptPass` (`SculptPass.cs:16`), which subclasses Pro's `VoxelGenerationPass`. The wishlist asked for a workflow that does not rely on play mode, and leaving the pipeline must not take the only tool away. So `GeoForge.Places` ships an edit-mode voxel brush: add, remove and paint material with a sphere, a box or a smoothing stroke, working on a scene GeoForge volume through GeoForge's existing terraform operations (`TerraformSphereCommand`, `TerraformCubeCommand`), with undo, and captured into the Place's voxel layer by the capture that already exists. Changing a stored layer is one action: **Edit terrain** on the voxel layer in the Place inspector stamps the layer into a scratch volume of its size in a temporary scene, opens the brush there, and captures back into the same layer when the designer closes it (or discards on cancel), so stamp, brush and capture are one click and never three manual steps. It is an editor action of the layer's owner, `GeoForge.Places`, with no command-line operation, because it is interactive. The scratch scene also holds the place's other layers read-only: each layer owner's stamper has a preview mode that places its props without colliders and that is never captured back, so the designer widening a tunnel sees the chest and the ore and does not bury them; and when the designer closes the brush, `PlacementsInRockCheck` runs on the new layer before the capture is accepted and lists every placement now sealed. It is an editor tool over operations GeoForge has, not a new data form: the layer is still the Place's own serialized fields. **Revising an approved place** is area 2's `core revise` on the Place, which copies it into the drafts folder. A layer whose data sits in its own asset (the voxel layer, if W4b's size measurement says its runs belong in a separate file) is revised by that asset's own `core revise`, whose answer names the layer and its owner; `core revise` does no more than that. The change itself is an edit operation of GeoForge.Places on the revision, `geoforge layer-edit`, a `Writes` operation into the drafts folder that applies the brush's own terraform commands (add, remove, smooth, with a sphere or a box) to the revised layer with no scene and no interactive step (argument names are area 2's), so an agent can change an approved cave and a person approves the result; Edit terrain stays the interactive road. It is folded into the brush story of W4b, so no story is added, and until W4b's measurement says whether a layer is its own asset the operation reads the layer where it sits. The brush ships in W4b and works on every new Place from then on. A point of interest exists once at a time: the 51 captures and the `SculptPass` recordings stay Saboteer's only copy, edited as today, until W10a, which in one sitting re-imports the captures as voxel layers, replays the recordings into them (no reader of the old recordings stays) and switches the floors, so no edit can land on a copy the game does not read and nothing is thrown away at the switch.

**Editing a place both games share.** A shared place lives in Studio.Content, which a game installs from a git URL and follows on a branch, and Unity treats such a package as immutable: "Most packages are immutable, including packages downloaded from the package registry or by Git URL" (Unity manual, embedded packages, 6000.4). Studio.Content is therefore edited in StudioDev only, never inside a game, and no game holds an embedded copy of a Studio package (decided 2026-10-24); Workflow's `check.sh` fails a game repository whose `Packages` folder holds a Studio package, so the guard is a check and not a habit. The loop: the designer opens the place in StudioDev, where the gym spawns the studio's playable character from Studio.Content's profile; runs Edit terrain and the gym there; pushes to StudioDev's repository; and presses Update in the game, where the place is then played with the game's own gym profile. This is by design, and it is the answer to the designer reader's finding that a shared place can only be edited in another project. No button in the Spellbound window edits a package, and no story of this area depends on one.

**The gym.** `places gym-start --target <asset>` plays one Place alone, or one prefab asset (an artist's prop, so it can be seen in the gym before it is committed), and returns while play mode starts; `places gym-status` answers `succeeded: gym loading`, then `succeeded: gym ready, character 'Prospector' at entrance`, or `failed: <reason>`; `places gym-leave` ends it; `--item` names an item the gym's character holds; when the prefab is a held prop (it has the grip node of a contract socket) the character holds it with Character's hold function, the one the game runs. W4c sits behind Saboteer's milestone and before any Corsairs launch (decided 2026-10-24); no art epic before A7 waits on it, A7 waits on W4c and not the other way, and the artist's last look before then is the hold test in the verdict window; until it lands a designer shapes terrain with Edit terrain (W4b) and judges a place with the checks, without the gym. The gym character is always a playable character of the game the place is in, chosen in three layers: Places ships a neutral default profile in Runtime that spawns the prefab the project names in Places' settings asset and loads no commands, so a stranger's project has a gym on day one; Studio.Content's profile replaces it and offers the studio's playable characters; a game's own profile wins inside that game's project. With the default active and no prefab named, `places gym-start` answers `refused: no gym character is set` then `Set 'Gym Character Prefab' on the Places settings asset` (area 2's two-line refusal). Places depends on Core alone, so it cannot tell a playable character from any other prefab; Studio.Content's profile depends on Character and lists only prefabs whose controller component carries the empty marker interface `IPlayableCharacter`, which `CharacterKit.IsPlayable` answers (decided 2026-10-24), and the default's field is labelled for one. When the gym has loaded the place, Places raises a place-loaded event naming the place and its world instance, which `Navigation.Places` uses to build the gym's field. Whichever profile is active gives the spawned character a stable name (the one `gym-status` prints), so a scenario line can address it. The soak and the gym use the same body, the prefab the project names through Places' neutral default profile, a playable character of the game; no stand-in character is spawned by Places or by the soak, the soak's fault matrix includes Runtime's hitch case (R3d) and a linked move under latency (R7b) run on that body, and until W4c lands the soak reads the project's playable character prefab from its own field. Places hands the item to the profile and knows nothing of hands. All three operations are shared, never per game.

**Authoring contract and validation.** The sealed-deposit error of the cave walk has an owner: `PlacementsInRockCheck`, a `IDefinitionCheck<PlaceDefinition>` in the editor assembly of the `GeoForge.Places` bridge, because that bridge is the one package that knows both the voxel layer and, through Places' read-only points surface, where the object layers put things. It asks Core's `IOccupancySource`, which the voxel layer implements over its own runs with no stamp and no scene, to classify a box of 1.5 m (a field) around each placement point; `solid` means no voxel in the box is open, so it reports `error geoforge.inside-rock deposits[7]` with the hint `move it or carve to it`. The point surface is read-only members Places offers, the same reading `ReachableDepositsCheck` uses, so Places learns nothing of voxels and imports nothing. It compiles wherever Places and GeoForge are installed, so Saboteer has it and Navigation is not needed. The finding code and the check's row in the table of checks are area 2's, and `UI.md` draws the same code. The other error of the walk, a layer that opens in 0 percent of sampled columns, belongs to a world definition and not to a place: it is the `WorldDefinition`'s own `Validate` (W1 story 12). Places, GeoForge and WorldForge definitions implement Core's `IAuthoredDefinition` and carry no format version; a changed layout means re-author, and a stored field the type no longer has is an error that `core validate` reports by name (Core.Editor's authored-key scan, which resolves managed references by their stored type names and so reaches a Place's layer list). A layer owner may mark an object layer harvestable. When Navigation is installed, its `ReachableDepositsCheck` in `Navigation.Places.Editor`, a `IDefinitionCheck<PlaceDefinition>` registered through Core.Editor's check seam, adds a finding to Places' report: a deposit on a harvestable layer that no walker can reach is an error, `failed: layer 'rare_ore' is harvestable and 11 of 14 deposits cannot be reached from the entrance (first: deposits[2])`. The registered names, which area 2's table of checks now lists: `geoforge.placements-in-rock (GeoForge)` for `PlacementsInRockCheck` (finding `geoforge.inside-rock`) and `places.layer-owner-installed (Places)` for `LayerOwnerInstalledCheck` (findings `places.layer-carried` and `places.layer-not-installed`), both over `PlaceDefinition` and both with `RunsWhileEditing` yes, because each is a lookup (one 1.5 m box per placement against the layer's own runs, and Unity's missing-type list) and not a build; neither is timed, CL4 story 13 times them, and the host moves one that goes over budget off the banner path. The finding codes carry the prefix of the package that raises them, as area 2 spells them: `geoforge.inside-rock` from GeoForge's check, `places.layer-carried` and `places.layer-not-installed` from Places'. The object layer's check is an `IDefinitionCheck<PlaceDefinition>` named `PresetReferencesCheck` (registered as `worldobjects.preset-references (WorldObjects)`, `RunsWhileEditing` yes as area 2 proposes) in the editor assembly of the `WorldObjects.Places` bridge: it resolves every preset id the layer names against the preset registry. Places' owner exposes `PlaceDefinition` to checks only through that seam and adds no hook of its own; its public surface offers the harvestable layers, the entrance and the rules as read-only members, with the default movement profile held as a name Places stores and does not interpret, so `Navigation.Places.Editor` compiles against it (W4a).

**Reference frames.** A stamp may name a reference frame (the default is the world, `ReferenceFrameId` zero in Core). An object layer stamped into a reference frame hands WorldObjects reference-frame-local records, which WorldObjects partitions by reference frame and cell with the pose relative to the reference frame. A voxel layer cannot be stamped into a reference frame, because a GeoForge volume is in the world reference frame; the stamp is refused with a finding.

**Fixed and rolled parts.** A designer fixes the entrance room, rolls the middle rooms and limits the treasure room to the back third. The arrangement the composer takes carries a rule per cell: a fixed part, a weighted pool of parts, or an allowed region (a set of cells, so "the back third" is a region and not a coordinate). The composer honours every rule: fixed cells are placed first, pools and regions narrow what the adjacency solver may choose, and a contradiction is reported with the cell that caused it and retried with the next seed up to an attempt count that is a field. The Place inspector shows the cells as a grid, colours fixed, pooled and free cells, and rerolls the seed in edit mode so the designer sees what chance does inside their constraints. Part, pool and region ids are open id sets, like sockets. All of it is data on the arrangement asset; nobody writes a composer.

**Duplication.** Layers refer to the Place's own children (sub-places, parts, other layers) through a table the Place owns, and a layer stores an index into it. Duplicate rewrites the table, so it renames every reference without reading any layer, including a layer whose owner package is not installed. References from a layer to shared assets outside the Place (a scatter layer's prefabs) are deliberately not renamed: the copy shares them, and the duplicate report lists every layer carried opaque with the owner package it needs.

### The handover, in numbers a designer can set

Horizon is told one thing about the voxels: which areas are covered. It hides its own terrain where meshes exist and fades inside a band where they are about to. The far surface sits slightly below the voxel surface by the depth bias, as today. Marching-cubes surface and a sampled height differ by up to about half a voxel plus whatever players dug, so the band is where the two meet and the skirt covers any gap. Edits do not appear in the far field unless GeoForge publishes a coarse edit summary per chunk (a height delta and a solid flag per cell) that Horizon applies; the earlier revision computed 1,024 cells per chunk column block, a few kilobytes. That is an optional story in W7 (computed, not built); without it a hole dug 300 units away is invisible from 3,000.

## Directions

**A. WorldForge as the world engine (rejected).** The earlier pitch's shape: far field, content approval, maps, navigation graph, sharding and a new file format inside WorldForge. It is the alternative to separating create, apply and render. Rejected: it mixes thirteen concerns (the evidence file lists them), a stranger who wants only the far field or only places would install the whole engine, and each of its parts has an owner on the approved line.

**B. The world is a function and every package is a reader (taken).** WorldForge creates; GeoForge, WorldObjects, Instancing, Navigation and Horizon apply or draw; Places holds authored units. Joins are the seams above. Taken all the way in this file.

**C. Voxels to the horizon (rejected).** The alternative to a separate far field: extend GeoForge's octree so far chunks are coarse voxels. Rejected by the code: levels of detail stride over the full array (`MarchingCubeJob.cs:75,103`), so the data stays at one-unit resolution at any distance, about 80 GiB for one dense chunk per column of a 10,000-unit world (computed), and the developer's own judgement is that coarse voxels look poor at range. GeoForge's stride LODs remain right for near and mid range.

**D. Generation stays in GeoForge Pro (rejected).** The alternative to WorldForge owning generation: Pro's pipeline becomes the generator and WorldForge only plans. Rejected: GeoForge's sentence is density in, meshes out; a free GeoForge user and the racing game would have to buy Pro to generate anything; and Pro's pipeline is single-chunk, synchronous and has no notion of a world plan, biomes or other readers.

**F. Generators stay in Saboteer's repository (rejected).** The alternative to Studio.Content: Corsairs could not nest a board and the showpiece leaves the roadmap. Rejected by the developer's answer to question 5.

**E. The far field's technique (none chosen; planned, then measured).** Three renderers behind one seam: today's tiles moved into Horizon (known), nested rings of one grid whose heights come from a texture (constant cost at any world size, after Losasso and Hoppe, "Geometry clipmaps", SIGGRAPH 2004), and Unity's own Terrain (requirement 4: Unity's feature wins unless shown not good enough). Terrain's heightmap resolution is clamped to 33 up to 4097 samples per side (Unity scripting reference, `TerrainData.heightmapResolution`) and holes are a boolean array set with `SetHoles` (`TerrainData.SetHoles`), so a 20,000-unit world would be several Terrains and a hole map driven by coverage events. Nothing here says it fails; nothing says it passes. W6.1 writes the protocol, the machine, the world sizes (a 10,000-unit and a 40,000-unit radius) and the pass numbers down before anything is built; W6.2 measures all three against them; the page chooses after.

## Ideas, kept and cut

Kept, with where each lands:

1. The world as a function read at a footprint, so near and far agree by construction. (WorldForge, Core seam)
2. Conservative occupancy: skip empty and solid chunks before paying for them. (Core seam, GeoForge, Horizon)
3. Crossing surfaces as the shape, an ordered stack along the frame's up. (WorldForge)
4. Every operator and rule declares its influence bounds, which gives occupancy, plan-once and the chunk-local rule of the performance contract. (WorldForge)
5. Placement ids derived from rule, cell and ordinal, so the same record is the same object on every peer and across saves. (WorldObjects, WorldForge)
6. One raster serves the seed preview, the minimap, the world map and Horizon's heights. (WorldForge)
7. Labels instead of water: a named surface the game reads. (WorldForge)
8. A location becomes a rule that points at a Place; the Place holds the content. (WorldForge, Places)
9. Horizon's handover driven by coverage events plus a configurable band. (Horizon)
10. A world fingerprint saved with the world, so a package update that changes a seed's output is detected, not silent. (WorldForge, Persistence)
11. `Explain(position)`: which surfaces, operators and rules produced this point. (WorldForge)
12. Golden worlds: one neutral definition shipped in the package and the Corsairs definition in the game, hashed by region. (WorldForge tests, Core harness)
13. Place voxel data as serialized fields in the asset, with today's 63.7 MiB of text as the baseline to measure against, and packed only when a Place is saved or sent. (Places, GeoForge.Places)
14. Counts, not costs, from WorldForge: each reader prices its own memory and time through Core's observations. (WorldForge, Core)

## Limits

- Horizon draws one surface per column, the top one; floating land needs a second renderer that marches a density source with GeoForge's standalone march operation (W7, optional).
- The first release is planar, all of it: `WorldModel` stacks its surfaces along one reference frame's up and `ISurfaceSource` is a height field over one frame's plane. A world whose up varies per point (an ocean moon, a cave world curling round) is served by none of the surface readers (scatter, Navigation cost, crossing surfaces, Horizon); they refuse it instead of sampling a fixed axis. GeoForge itself and placement poses carry no up, so such a game can still fill a volume with its own density source. A curved-up surface seam would be a different seam, not a parameter of this one.
- The plan is built once for the whole world (28 ms at a 10,000-unit radius in the earlier pitch, under about 100 ms by the performance contract); a world of 100 km needs the plan by region, which is not designed here.
- Far-field speed, memory and seam quality are unmeasured; W6's spike decides.
- The 70 to 85 percent of chunks skippable is the earlier pitch's estimate; W2 counts them.
- Edits show in the far field only after W7's optional edit-summary story.
- Float equality across CPUs rests on Burst's deterministic mode, whose manual gives no cross-platform results. Unity says floating-point results may differ between Mono and CoreCLR and that Burst is not affected (UnityChanges.md, page 15); WorldForge's generation runs in Burst jobs and a search of its runtime found no `GetHashCode` call, so a difference is expected only in managed code, and W1's compare story measures it.
- The compressed size of a voxel chunk, the file size of a voxel layer as serialized fields and the packed size of a Place are unmeasured; today's 63.7 MiB for the 51 captures is the baseline and W4b measures first. The dense figure is arithmetic.
- Server collision and residency are decided here (the presenter split and Decision 5); the chunk count and march cost they imply are measured by W2 and reported through Networking's capacity report.
- Whether Physics.BakeMesh in the march chain gains anything is unmeasured; W2 measures before it changes the chain.
- Horizon's advantage over Unity Terrain is a hypothesis with four testable parts until W6.
- The `LodTarget` error without a camera was claimed by another area; I confirmed where it is referenced, not its error path.
- The world is bounded: the plan covers it whole until backlog item B1.
- One class byte per sample: a game that needs a second continuous channel (moisture) adds a second source under its own seam, or asks Core's review for a channel.
